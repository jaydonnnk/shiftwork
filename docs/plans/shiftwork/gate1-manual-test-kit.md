# Gate 1 Manual Test Kit: Shiftwork

**Status:** revised 2026-09-24 for the final-pivot Gate 1 draft; still unrun.
The 2026-09-15 kit was not run, and its later gate approval supplied no human
results. This revision has not been approved. Formative comprehension testing is
not proof of learning improvement or a measurement of the 20-point north star.

## Purpose and limits

Use five people with basic coding knowledge if feasible. Ask whether the six
screens make investigation, fair comparison, explicit choice and inheritance
understandable. The user runs any participant sessions; this kit authorizes no
outreach. Browser checks or agent reviews never count as participants.

The main failure risks are a pre-solved diagnosis, predictions that feel like
paperwork, an obvious winning repair, and confusion between experiments and
operational history. A practitioner should separately assess the incident and
Northline transfer task. A strong course plus a debugger is a credible alternative;
ask what this integrated journey adds rather than assuming it is unique.

## Setup

- Open `mockups/01-shift-board.html` in a browser or use a local static server.
  The mockups use plain HTML/CSS; no service runs or choices persist.
- One participant at a time, about 20 to 30 minutes. Record actual duration.
- Explain only that it is a non-saving storyboard. Preview links show authored
  examples, not the participant's saved decisions. Do not otherwise explain the product.
- Read each task as written. Use neutral prompts such as "What makes you say that?"
  Do not supply a diagnosis or recommend a repair.
- Record verbatim quotes and observed actions, including confusion and incomplete
  tasks. Do not paraphrase observations into pass language.
- Record any eventual results in `00-status.md`, keeping simulated, browser and
  human evidence separate. Do not fill the recording sheet with imagined results.

## Tests, tasks and pass conditions

### 1. Product comprehension

- Task: show the board for ten seconds. Ask: "What are you responsible for, and why
  would you return?"
- Pass: at least 4/5 describe investigating and maintaining the same AI service
  across later shifts, rather than chatting with a tutor or managing a business game.

### 2. Diagnosis before help

- Task: inspect Request 1 without opening the optional diagnostic example. Ask:
  "What might explain this response? What result would show your explanation is
  wrong? What would you change or hold constant to test that?"
- Pass: at least 4/5 identify an observation, a tentative cause and a test that
  could contradict it. Naming the intended repair is not required. Merely writing
  an answer or repeating the screen's phrasing does not satisfy the condition.
- Watch for: treating the supported £18 price as successful completion of the
  collection and alternative request, or accepting a model's diagnosis as a verdict.

### 3. Fair comparison and genuine choice

- Task: compare A and B. Ask: "What makes these results comparable? What would
  invalidate the comparison? Which would you keep, and what are you giving up?"
- Pass: at least 4/5 identify shared requirements and starting conditions, the
  difference between recorded inspection and fresh trials, and one real tradeoff.
  They must notice repeats/variation and distinguish useful answers from referrals.
- Ask: "Would a cheaper repair that breaks a mandatory rule be eligible? Would
  refusing every request be success?" Everyone must answer no and explain why.
- Neither A nor B is the answer key. Record rationales, not preferred-option counts.

### 4. Experiment, choose and operate

- Task: ask which actions can change the ongoing incident and ledger, which exact
  version returns, and what happens if another candidate is saved later.
- Pass: at least 4/5 explain that experiments retain evidence without changing
  operational history, choice binds an exact version, and operation records
  consequences. A later save must not silently replace the chosen version.
- Ask what the mockup's links actually do. Everyone must understand they navigate
  authored examples and do not save a choice or execute a service.

### 5. Later consequences without hindsight

- Task: choose either independent history preview. Inspect its later outage and
  alternative experiment. Ask: "Does this make your original choice wrong? Can
  trying the alternative erase what happened? What would you reconsider now?"
- Pass: at least 4/5 judge the original choice using its original evidence, keep
  the two histories separate, and explain that a later experiment cannot rewrite
  operational consequences or automatically select a new operating version.

### 6. Authority and evidence

- Task: ask who owns explanations, code, experiments, choices, assistance and
  independent assessment. Ask which displayed results are measured.
- Pass: everyone identifies the learner's decision ownership, independent
  assessment and the illustrative nature of all mockup results. Models cannot
  declare passes or inspect protected answers. Synthetic outcomes are not revenue,
  learning gains or job-readiness evidence. Systematic confusion blocks approval.

### 7. Reduced-assistance transfer and incident quality

- Learner task: inspect Northline. Ask: "What reasoning can you reuse, and what
  must you work out again?"
- Learner pass: at least 4/5 distinguish inherited Bellwether code from a fresh
  Northline artifact and name a new commitment, safety or retry-related problem.
- Practitioner task: assess whether Bellwether offers plausible competing
  explanations and repairs, and Northline requires new reasoning.
- Practitioner pass: explicitly identify defensible tradeoffs and a distinguishing
  experiment. Fail if the mission is a hidden prescribed answer or a renamed lab.

### 8. Motivation and cognitive load

- Task: ask whether they would voluntarily return without badges or credentials,
  which input helped their next action, and which felt like unnecessary paperwork.
- Pass: at least 3/5 want to return for investigation or continuing consequences;
  at least 4/5 explain how their prediction changed the test they would run.
- Record objections to the product rather than attributing them to the participant.

### 9. Accessibility sanity

- Task: keyboard-only navigation and disclosure, 200% zoom, and a roughly
  390px-wide view. Read the comparison and reach both choice previews.
- Pass: logical focus order, visible focus, labelled inputs, readable tables,
  usable actions and no horizontal page scrolling or pointer-only stations.
- Browser inspection can verify layout mechanics, not human comprehension.

## Decision rule

- Apply individual conditions as written; do not average away a failed condition.
- Systematic authority/evidence confusion blocks approval. Concrete product failures
  in diagnosis, comparison, selection or incident authenticity require revision.
- Record any product-level failure and keep Gate 1 in progress, revise the artifacts,
  then ask: "Approve Gate 1, or what should change?"
- If the user elects to approve without human sessions, record that explicitly;
  every unrun threshold remains unmet. This optional kit is not an invented new
  mandatory approval gate.
- A pass does not prove learning, commercial value, employer acceptance or the
  north-star target. Study design remains a separate later-gate decision.
