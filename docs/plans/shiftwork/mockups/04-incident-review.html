Observed failure · storyboard

The service answered from yesterday’s stock.

It told a customer that one copy remained after the final copy had already sold. New stock is data; ignoring freshness is the reusable procedural failure.

Evidence available to you

Customer output
“Yes, one paperback is available.”
Current shop record
Available copies: 0
Committed stance violated
Freshness-first — records must be verified before answering.

Choose the repair direction

Several repairs are defensible; each trades safety, speed and usefulness differently, and the chosen repair must survive a later unseen case.

Verify freshness at answer time
Re-check the shop record before replying; refuse or ask when it is stale. Buys: closes the failure family. Costs: slower replies and more refusals.
Answer from the shop’s live record
Read current stock at request time instead of a cached copy. Buys: attacks the root cause. Costs: depends on a source you do not control; a silent sync failure is a new failure family.
Stop claiming exact stock
Say “recently showed N available” with the record’s age. Buys: stays fast and helpful. Costs: softens a hard error into a caveat a customer may ignore.

The storyboard continues with freshness verification; other repairs change what the later shifts test.
