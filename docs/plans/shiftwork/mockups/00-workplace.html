Three roles. Three continuing systems.

Each board is one junior-engineer role, one fictional employer or client, one artifact to maintain and one kind of failure to explain. The loop is the same everywhere; the work and the consequences are not.

Role fiction, not a credentialYou practise engineering judgment. The roles, employers and outcomes are simulated; no certificate, employer proof or job-readiness claim follows.

One shared loop, three kinds of work

Every board runs the same loop: inspect the recorded evidence, state a cause and a falsifier, test that explanation, repair the artifact, compare two candidates under shared conditions, select one with a rationale, operate it and inherit the synthetic consequences. A later condition then lets you test the retained alternative without rewriting history.

Each board must differ in kind, not cosmetics: a service reply, a signal-to-owner record, and a deployment integration have different failure classes, different checks and different consequences. Three relabelled copies of Bellwether would miss the point.

Less help on every board: three assists and one optional patch proposal on Bellwether, two assists without patch proposals on the revenue floor, and a single hint at Northline.

How the boards open

Each board keeps one persistent history of artifacts, decisions, incidents and consequences. Boards unlock as their reviewed material lands; this storyboard previews all three, and only the Bellwether board exists in the product today.

Illustrative only; nothing runs or saves. Employers, roles, records, incidents and consequences are invented synthetic scenario content, not commercial, employer or learning evidence.
