Deployment brief · Conflicting requirements

What the co-op needs, and what the template assumes

1

Speed at the counter versus safe commitments

Counter staff want confirmation without leaving the queue. Co-op policy: never confirm a repair before parts and mechanic availability are checked.

2

Safety questions go to a mechanic

A customer asks whether an uninspected brake fault is safe to ride with. The integration must escalate to a human mechanic and never answer from a knowledge base.

3

Retries must not double-book

The booking widget retries after connection failures. The integration must keep one intended repair slot without duplicating a booking or losing the first commitment.

The vendor's default template assumes immediate confirmation and cannot escalate; the co-op will not accept that. Pilot rollout requires the customer acceptance checks below to pass and a rollout and handover decision to be recorded.

System map · Discovery record and permissions

What the integration may touch

Booking surface
The co-op's booking widget sends each request to the integration and retries it on failure.
Availability data
Parts stock and mechanic schedules change through the day; nothing is confirmed from memory.
Safety policy
No assurance without a mechanic inspection; the integration escalates instead of answering.
Permissions map
Read parts availability and mechanic schedules; write repair slots only. No payment, account or customer-history access.
Recorded conflict
The template's fast path contradicts the co-op's policy, and both cannot win.

Compare two ways to keep the counter honest

integration codeacceptance testspermissions mapdiscovery record

The artifact is the integration itself: validation, escalation and retry-safe writes. The starter template exists; no candidate is preselected.

A · optimistic auto-confirm with rollback

Confirm at the counter immediately, watch for conflicts and roll back what fails. Fastest for customers, but every retraction needs a counter follow-up and the rollback window will not always stay invisible.

B · conservative validation with manual fallback

Check parts and mechanic availability before confirming, and hand anything unclear to the counter. Never retracts, but the queue waits on every confirmation.

Acceptance · Independently checked

The co-op's acceptance tests

  • No double bookings. One intended slot per request, under retries and failures.
  • Escalation fires. Every safety question reaches a human mechanic before any answer is given.
  • Commitments honored. A confirmed slot stays; the co-op records any change and why. A rollback resolved within the same counter visit counts as a recorded follow-up, not a missed commitment.
  • Access boundaries held. Availability is read and slots are written; payment and account data are never touched.

The independent evaluator owns these checks; learner code and assistance cannot alter them, and the protected workload stays sealed. Visible acceptance tests mirror them for your own runs. A failing check blocks selection regardless of speed or cost.

Rollout · Pilot state

Operate the choice, then live with it

Operation writes the pilot state: bookings, incidents, and the rollout and handover decisions the co-op records. An experiment never changes the pilot; only operation does. Each kept example below is a separate invented history.

Example history: kept A · optimistic auto-confirm

Invented post-operation illustration. This is not your selection and nothing runs.

Operated A on the pilot: 15 confirmations landed within seconds and 3 safety questions escalated; 2 counter follow-ups were recorded. Later condition in this example: the co-op renames an availability field during a booking-schema update and a customer-side connection drops mid-shift. A's optimistic path confirms two requests against the stale field shape and retracts both within the same counter visit; NB-01 records the mapping defect, not a customer harm.

Example history: kept B · conservative validation

Invented post-operation illustration. This is not your selection and nothing runs.

Operated B on the pilot: 15 confirmations and 3 escalations with no retractions; the queue carried the wait. Later condition in this example: the same schema update and connection drop pause confirmations; every affected request waits in the fallback queue, nothing is confirmed from the stale shape, and the queue clears once the mapping is repaired.

Retained alternative experiment: after the later condition, the alternative is tested under that same condition as a separate, non-writing experiment. A different operating integration is a new explicit action with a new rationale; nothing switches automatically and nothing rewrites what the chosen version did.

Inheritance: bookings, incidents, rollout decisions and the retained alternative persist. The next shift inherits the exact selected integration, not the newest saved file.

Handover: what the co-op receives

The runbook written from your discovery record, the permissions map, the acceptance results, the known limits (the retraction window for A, or the wait for B) and the unresolved incidents. The co-op owns the rollout decision; you record the handover.
