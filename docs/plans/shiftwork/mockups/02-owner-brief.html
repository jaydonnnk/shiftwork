Before the first shift

Decide what your service must never get wrong.

The owner needs helpful answers, but a confident invention is worse than an honest refusal.

Owner request

“Help customers find books, check price and availability, and understand our returns policy.”

Hard boundary
Never invent price, availability or a policy exception.
Useful behaviour
Ask a clarifying question or decline when evidence is insufficient.

Your correctness stance

No default is applied. Your commitment is recorded and inherited by later shifts.

Freshness-first
Answer only from records verified at answer time. Buys: no stale answers. Costs: more refusals and slower replies.
Helpful with labels
Use the newest record, show its age and label uncertainty. Buys: more completed requests. Costs: a customer may act on stale data anyway.
Exact where it matters
Hard checks for price and stock; judgement elsewhere. Buys: a middle path. Costs: deciding what “matters” is itself a decision you must defend.

Storyboard preview continues with freshness-first; other stances produce different incidents.
