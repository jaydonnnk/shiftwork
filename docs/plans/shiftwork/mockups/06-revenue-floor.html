Qualification rules · Illustrative proposed contract

What the workflow must do, for both candidates

  • Exactly one owner per signal. Every signal ends owned under the pinned rules; nothing is left unowned or assigned to a guess.
  • Uncertain records go to the review queue. Low confidence is not a reason to skip qualification; anything unclear waits for a person.
  • Reprocessing is idempotent. A repeated delivery updates the existing record; it never creates a second one.
  • SLA clocks start once, when a record is owned. Duplicate clocks distort the pipeline and are checked.
  • A human handoff, never a sent message. The workflow prepares work for a person; the artifact never contacts a customer.

These rules are visible, not hidden grading rules. You can add learner-owned tests and priorities; you cannot waive a mandatory rule. Replacing an unnecessary AI step with ordinary code remains a valid choice.

Workflow bench · Two candidates, one contract

Compare two ways to own every record

A · rules-first

Deterministic qualification and a routing table, with a review queue for anything that does not map cleanly. Predictable ownership at the lowest cost, but more records wait for a person.

B · model-assisted

Model-assisted qualification with confidence thresholds, sending uncertain records to the same review queue. Records reach owners faster, at higher cost and with a threshold to maintain.

Illustrative directions, not supplied executable patches. The intended work is changing your own workflow code and visible tests.

Pipeline wall · Operation and inheritance

Operate the choice, then live with it

Operation writes synthetic pipeline state: owners, SLA clocks, escalations and missed-handoff incidents. An experiment never moves an owner or starts a clock; only operation does. Each kept example below is a separate invented history, not a combined ledger and not your saved selection.

Example history: kept A · rules-first

Invented post-operation illustration. This is not your selection and nothing runs.

Operated A on the next shift's signals: every record ended owned once; 3 records waited in the review queue; R-104 was re-owned to the Enterprise pod and the duplicate retired. SB-01 resolves only when linked operational evidence satisfies its resolution rule; choosing a repair did not close it.

Later condition in this example: the enrichment provider is unavailable. A still qualifies against the pinned rules; review-queue arrivals rise from 3 to 6, and time-to-owner for clean records is unchanged.

Example history: kept B · model-assisted

Invented post-operation illustration. This is not your selection and nothing runs.

Operated B on the next shift's signals: every record ended owned once; 2 handoffs; the fastest time-to-owner in the samples. SB-01's mis-route is corrected once the operated workflow re-owns R-104, and the incident resolves on that linked evidence.

Later condition in this example: the enrichment provider is unavailable. Confidence scoring degrades at the threshold boundary and 11 records fall back to the review queue; nothing ends unowned, and the speed advantage largely disappears.

Retained alternative experiment: after the later condition, the alternative is tested under that same condition as a separate, non-writing experiment. Choosing a different operating workflow is a new explicit action with a new rationale; nothing switches automatically and nothing rewrites what the chosen version did.

Inheritance: owners, SLA clocks, escalations, missed-handoff incidents, both candidates and the original rationale persist. The next shift inherits the exact selected workflow, not the newest saved file.
